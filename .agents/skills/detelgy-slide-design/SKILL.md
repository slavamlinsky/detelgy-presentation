# Detelgy Slide Design Workflow

## 1. Read the brief
- Read `AGENTS.md` and the three presentation content/design files referenced there.
- Identify the slide number and its single narrative job in the deck.
- Inspect its existing screenshot and component if available.
- Name its audience takeaway in one sentence before touching code.

## 2. Distill content
- Identify one headline (ideally short) and one optional support sentence.
- Keep no more than 3–5 primary visual concepts unless the material demands otherwise.
- Merge channels/examples into user-level categories; retain specifics in notes or hover details.
- Preserve critical distinctions between real features, roadmap, and hypotheses.

## 3. Pick one metaphor
- Choose a distinct visual metaphor that makes the slide understandable without reading labels.
- Approved motif examples: ascending path for growth, separated fragments for fragmentation, connected paths for journeys, growing network for relationships.
- Do NOT force the cover's ascending curve onto all slides.
- On fragmentation slides, elegant separated puzzle-like fragments may be tested, but avoid childish jigsaw art and don't imply existing professional organizations provide no value.

## 4. Compose
- Start with broad masses: headline area, focal artwork, and whitespace.
- Favor large type, clear hierarchy, and meaningful scale differences.
- Remove redundant footers, competing callouts, and repetitive ornaments.
- Keep brand recognition through logo, navy/sky color system, and illustration quality.
- Use semantic SVG layers for future motion; avoid baking key text into raster images.

## 5. Motion last
- First approve a still screenshot.
- Then, if requested, add restrained motion that reinforces reading order.
- Sequence: headline/support reveal -> concept build -> finishing focal emphasis.
- Avoid layout shifts, delayed legibility, ornamental loops, and hover-only essential meaning.
- Test hover and keyboard focus; support reduced motion and fully visible print/PDF.

## 6. Quality gate
1. Capture at 1920×1080.
2. Confirm the message can be identified within 3–5 seconds.
3. Check headline breaks, overlap, empty space, focal point, icon consistency, and visual weight.
4. Confirm responsive layout and no clipped text.
5. Export or inspect PDF rendering.
6. Summarize what was removed, what became more visually meaningful, and remaining uncertainties.
7. Stop after scoped slides and request design review before spreading changes.

## Lessons from reviewed slides

### Cover (Slide 01)
- Strong result: two-line `Where expertise becomes / opportunity.` with the last word highlighted.
- A rising path works when it communicates a concrete progression, not arbitrary orbits.
- Start -> Profile -> Connections -> Opportunities -> Growth is a possible semantic path. Labels should be short and mostly revealed on interaction, while icons have consistent visual style.
- Slightly increasing milestone sizes can imply progression; keep the final milestone largest but restrained.
- Preserve breathing room around big text and diagram.

### Fragmentation (Slide 02)
- Current seven-channel network felt noisy despite improved composition.
- Favor four broader dimensions: Visibility, Clients, Peers, Learning.
- Use separated visual fragments or islands around the expert; prioritize the fragmented *experience* over an inventory of channels.
- Avoid an overpowering source disclaimer in the main visual; maintain evidence and uncertainty in accessible speaker notes.
- Do not claim that existing networks are valueless or entirely disconnected; frame fragmentation as a hypothesis/experience worth validation.

## Delivery
Provide updated slide, screenshot(s), PDF check, and short design rationale. Do not rewrite strategic messaging without flagging it.
