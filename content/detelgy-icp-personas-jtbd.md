# Detelgy — ICP, Personas, Jobs To Be Done & Customer Journey

**Document type:** Strategic audience and user-value brief for stakeholder storytelling and Codex presentation production  
**Version:** Draft 1.0 — October 15, 2026  
**Owner:** Detelgy founding team  
**Evidence status:** Working hypotheses, not completed primary research. Personas are composites, not interviews with named people.  
**Companion:** `detelgy-product-brief.md`

## 1. Executive summary

Detelgy helps polygraph examiners turn professional expertise into **connections, a credible professional reputation, opportunities to grow, and potential earnings**. It serves professionals at different career stages rather than assuming every member wants more examinations.

**Primary ICP (initial commercial focus):** an independent or commercially active examiner who wants to grow a practice and professional visibility, is willing to maintain an online professional identity, and can benefit from client discovery, peer relationships, and reputation building.

**Two strategic complementary personas:** an established expert who can contribute knowledge, trust, consultations, mentorship, and community leadership; and an emerging professional who benefits from learning and professional belonging.

**User success dimensions:** expertise, recognition, meaningful connections, and income opportunities. The product can increase access to opportunities; it **cannot promise** higher individual earnings, expert quality, or marketplace demand.

## 2. Scope, audience sides and boundaries

Detelgy is a two-sided marketplace and a professional community. This brief intentionally focuses on the **professional/supply side**; buyers of examinations (individuals and organizations) require a separate demand-side ICP and customer journey before launching buyer acquisition campaigns.

The three professional personas are **behavioral archetypes**, not demographic market segments. Age, gender, nationality, and years in practice do not by themselves establish product fit. Illustrative names in presentations must be labeled *fictional composite persona*.

**Current starting geography:** Ukraine and Spain; growth markets are hypotheses to be prioritized through market validation. **Languages:** English, Spanish, Ukrainian and Russian, with real multilingual discovery and participation as a potential advantage.

**Reality check:** Community/discussions, expert profiles, some reviews, service listings and booking experience have been worked on. Large-scale marketplace liquidity, paid mentorship, structured Academy and profitable recurring revenue are **not demonstrated by this brief**. The deck must distinguish built features from planned services.

## 3. Market segmentation — by motivation and behavior

| Segment | Core motivation | Existing workaround | Detelgy entry value | Monetization potential | Strategic role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Growth-oriented independent examiner | Get quality leads and become more credible | Referrals, local website, social channels, personal network | Verified professional profile, community, discoverable services | Membership, commission on fulfilled services | **Primary ICP / initial supply** |
| Established specialist / recognized expert | Share expertise, influence standards, develop legacy, diversify work | Private referrals, association events, messaging groups, private teaching | Visible thought leadership, consultations, mentorship, articles | Paid peer services, courses, optional membership | **Trust and knowledge anchor** |
| Emerging or recently trained examiner | Gain confidence, reliable guidance, first connections | Training alumni chats, courses, asking known instructors | Questions, peers, mentorship, learning | Future upgrades, training and consultations | **Retention and future supply** |
| Small practice owner / team lead | Grow referrals and capacity; hire/collaborate | Agency website, ads, team contacts | Discoverability and partner relationships | Multi-user/team offering (future hypothesis) | Expansion segment |
| Educator / training organization | Reach learners and build a trusted audience | Conferences, websites, webinars | Co-created education and professional audience | Partnership and training revenue (future) | Partner segment |

**Exclusions / caution:** Avoid treating all police, government, or institutional examiners as available commercial service providers. Their ethical, employer, privacy, and procurement constraints may restrict participation.

## 4. ICP qualification: who is most likely to get value first?

**Primary ICP — Growth-Oriented Independent Examiner.** A person or small practice that meets several of the following:

1. Currently offers legal professional examination services to external clients or plans to do so soon.
2. Has credible qualifications and is willing to complete reasonable verification/onboarding.
3. Wants greater client visibility or a portable professional reputation.
4. Uses at least some digital tools but lacks an integrated professional presence.
5. Is open to professional networking, publishing, or peer learning.
6. Operates in an initial launch location or language where Detelgy can provide **density** of colleagues and/or realistic demand.
7. May pay for distribution, visibility, or operational value **after** tangible benefit is demonstrated.

**Poor initial fit:** Examiner prohibited from commercial engagements; professional unwilling to share any public professional information; person expecting guaranteed incoming clients before supply/demand exists.

**Buyer and payer are not always the same:** an examiner may subscribe; a client may pay for an examination; an established specialist may earn from mentorship. Codex must not collapse these into one revenue source.

## 5. Primary persona A — The Growth-Oriented Examiner

> **Fictional composite:** “Andrii”, independent examiner / small-practice practitioner; experience is illustrative, not a criterion.

**Situation today:** Has training and successful work, but new clients arrive unevenly through local referrals and existing contacts. Qualifications, testimonials, services, and contributions are scattered. Attending events or finding trusted colleagues takes effort. Marketing is another job on top of professional work.

**Functional goals:** Get more qualified inquiries; present services clearly; display verified credentials; build reusable proof of experience; reach relevant peers.

**Emotional goals:** Feel professionally recognized, confident in being selected, less dependent on a few referral relationships.

**Social goals:** Be known as a reliable colleague rather than simply a listing in a directory.

**Pain / trigger moments:** A quiet month of inquiries; a prospective buyer asks for credentials and testimonials; a colleague is needed for a complex case; moving to a new city; noticing other experts receive more public recognition.

**Today’s alternatives:** Personal website, local SEO, word-of-mouth, social pages, WhatsApp/Telegram/Facebook communities, association directories, training alumni networks.

**Current limitations (hypotheses):** Fragmented professional identity; little visibility beyond existing circle; limited measurable return from informal community activity; uncertain repeatable lead generation.

**Detelgy value:** A professional identity connected to services, verified qualifications, reviews, public contributions, local/global discovery, and an expert network.

**First-value moment:** Completes a credible profile and makes the first relevant professional connection, or receives a meaningful profile visit/qualified inquiry.

**Aha moment:** First **qualified** client inquiry or valuable referral from outside the person's prior network, attributable to Detelgy. Not merely a page view.

**Long-term success:** More high-quality opportunities, a more resilient network, growing credible reputation, and (if demand materializes) increased/expanded earnings.

**Likely objections:** “Will buyers actually come?” “Why is this better than my website?” “How much time will I need to participate?” “How do I know the other profiles are genuine?”

**Jobs to be done:**
- *When potential clients compare practitioners,* help me communicate why my services fit their needs, **so I can earn their confidence without manually explaining everything each time**.
- *When referrals slow down,* help suitable new customers discover and contact me, **so my practice is less dependent on existing relationships**.
- *When I face an unfamiliar challenge,* help me reach competent peers, **so I can make better professional decisions and continue learning**.

## 6. Persona B — The Established Expert / Industry Leader

> **Fictional composite:** “Olena”, respected practitioner, trainer, or former high-volume examiner.

**Situation today:** Accumulated experience, a trusted circle, and expertise that colleagues seek; may no longer want a packed schedule of examinations. Knowledge is often shared privately, locally, or through occasional events.

**Functional goals:** Reach qualified peers; publish insights; select manageable advisory work; mentor upcoming examiners; help advance the profession.

**Emotional and social goals:** Meaningful recognition, professional legacy, intellectual exchange, impact beyond one country.

**Trigger moments:** Repeated inbound questions from less experienced examiners; invitation to speak or train; desire to reduce examination hours; desire to connect with peers in other countries.

**Alternatives:** Professional associations, own website, training center, webinars/conferences, private consultations, established reputation.

**Detelgy value:** Industry Leader recognition, reputation-rich profile, publications, roundtables, professional peer consulting and mentorship (where launched).

**First-value moment:** A relevant introduction to another respected expert or genuinely useful discussion with qualified colleagues.

**Aha moment:** A peer seeks out their specific expertise and a valued interaction occurs — not necessarily a paid transaction. For commercially active mentors, a completed paid consultation is a later monetization milestone.

**Long-term success:** Visible influence, international professional connections, meaningful knowledge contribution, optionally new revenue from expertise.

**Likely objections:** “Why should I contribute?” “Will discussion quality be high?” “Will my name be used as endorsement?” “Who moderates and protects confidentiality?”

**Jobs to be done:**
- *When I have knowledge worth passing on,* help it reach serious professionals, **so my work has a wider and lasting impact**.
- *When colleagues seek my advice,* provide a credible setting for consultations or mentorship, **so I can help selectively and sustainably**.
- *When I want to collaborate internationally,* introduce capable peers, **so we can exchange perspectives beyond our local circles**.

**Important design implication:** **Industry Leader is recognition, not automatically a marketplace performance level.** A leader who does not fulfill client orders must not appear inferior solely because of order counters. Peer consulting and mentoring should not be silently counted as polygraph examinations.

## 7. Persona C — The Emerging Professional

> **Fictional composite:** “María”, recently trained examiner building a practice.

**Situation today:** Has training but fewer professional relationships, limited practice visibility, and questions that may feel difficult to ask publicly. Finds resources across separate training programs and informal chats.

**Functional goals:** Access reliable guidance, meet peers, deepen competence, understand professional standards, grow an ethical practice and portfolio.

**Emotional and social goals:** Feel welcome and capable; gain confidence through genuine feedback; belong to a professional community.

**Trigger moments:** Recently completed training; received an unfamiliar case inquiry; needs to evaluate continuing education; wants first professional referrals.

**Alternatives:** Instructors, local associations, training classmates, webinars, group chats.

**Detelgy value:** Discussions, expert Q&A, roundtables, introductions, future mentoring and structured Academy.

**First-value moment:** Receives a respectful, substantive response or joins a discussion that resolves a professional question.

**Aha moment:** Develops an ongoing peer/mentor connection that enables better professional decisions and next steps.

**Long-term success:** Better competence, stronger professional relationships, credible identity, and eventual access to suitable commercial opportunities.

**Likely objections:** “Will experienced examiners respond?” “Can I safely ask a beginner question?” “Is it affordable?” “Are resources in my language?”

**Jobs to be done:**
- *When I am uncertain about a professional issue,* help me get constructive guidance, **so I can progress responsibly**.
- *When I have few contacts in the field,* help me form trusted relationships, **so I do not need to figure everything out alone**.
- *When I prepare to serve clients,* help me understand how to build credibility, **so I can develop my practice with appropriate standards**.

## 8. JTBD architecture — four common progress jobs

| Universal job | Functional progress | Emotional progress | Detelgy mechanisms | Useful signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Help me become better** | Learn, ask, obtain peer input | Confidence and competence | Discussions, events, consultations, future Academy | Valuable answers, useful sessions, learning completion |
| **Help me become known** | Present expertise and contributions | Recognition and credibility | Profiles, qualifications, articles, reviews, participation | Discoverability and meaningful engagement |
| **Help me find opportunities** | Receive relevant introductions/inquiries | Agency and professional momentum | Directory, service pages, marketplace, networking | Qualified inquiries, bookings, referrals |
| **Help me monetize what I know** | Offer exams, advice, mentorship, teaching | Independence and reward | Marketplace, consultations, future education | Completed paid transactions and net earnings |

A user may be motivated by more than one job; the dominant job can change throughout their career. **No job implies a guaranteed outcome.**

## 10. Customer journey — from first contact to professional success

| Stage | User question | Experience / touchpoint | Desired user outcome | Signal | Friction or risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Discover** | “Is this for professionals like me?” | Invite, colleague, LinkedIn, search, Industry Leader | Relevant reason to explore | Qualified landing visit | Too broad positioning |
| **Consider** | “Can I trust this?” | Examples, leadership, visible expert profiles, public discussions | Confidence to request access | Access request | Empty ecosystem / low trust |
| **Join** | “How much work is setup?” | Access approval, verification, onboarding | Usable professional identity | Profile activated | Complex forms / privacy concerns |
| **First value** | “Does this help me now?” | Meaningful discussion, helpful introduction, service listing | Tangible connection, guidance, or discoverability | First valuable interaction | Cold-start / unanswered post |
| **Engage** | “Is it worth returning?” | Topics, peers, events, useful notifications | Repeat professional benefit | Meaningful monthly activity | Spam / shallow conversation |
| **Build reputation** | “Does my contribution compound?” | Content, experience, reviews and feedback | Visible professional standing | Profile engagement, trusted signals | Inflated/unverifiable claims |
| **Create opportunity** | “Can this advance my practice?” | Relevant introductions, buyer inquiries, peer consulting | Useful professional or commercial opportunity | Qualified inquiry / engagement | Weak marketplace demand |
| **Earn / grow** | “Is this producing results?” | Fulfilled bookings, paid sessions, course participation | Revenue or demonstrated growth | Net earnings, completed engagements | Fees, cancellations, trust |
| **Lead / advocate** | “Can I help shape this community?” | Publications, mentorship, leadership, referrals | Influence, belonging and legacy | Mentoring, referrals, contributions | Over-commercialization |

**Journeys differ:** emerging professionals may achieve the first meaningful value through answers; leaders through peer connections; commercial examiners through qualified demand. Do **not** imply every user follows a linear funnel or reaches paid transactions.

## 11. Persona-specific activation and Aha Moments

| Persona | Activation metric (candidate) | Aha moment (candidate) | Sustained success (candidate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth-oriented examiner | Verified profile + published service | First attributable qualified lead outside existing network | Repeat qualified leads / fulfilled orders + positive reputation signals |
| Established expert | Completed profile + meaningful content/session | Sought-after peer interaction or invitation to collaborate | Repeated valued consultations, mentoring, influence |
| Emerging professional | Profile + first substantive Q&A interaction | Useful response that leads to ongoing peer/mentor relationship | Repeat learning and professional network growth |

**Distinguish activity from value:** profile creation is activation, not proof of value; views are not qualified leads; badge awards are not evidence of service quality; revenue without satisfaction is not durable success.

## 12. Professional success paths (nonexclusive)

1. **Practitioner:** Improve discoverability → earn buyer trust → receive appropriate inquiries → complete services → accumulate authentic reviews and relationships.
2. **Expert contributor:** Help peers → publish high-value knowledge → grow reputation → collaborate and optionally consult.
3. **Mentor / leader:** Share hard-won expertise → facilitate learning and introductions → create professional impact → optionally earn from structured advisory/education work.

These are **paths, not ranks**. Detelgy should avoid equating community status with clinical/scientific validity or conflating review quantity with excellence.

## 13. Economic model from the professional perspective

**Baseline (simplified):** income mainly from personally performed services; constrained by availability, locally accessible clients, and demand.

**Potential Detelgy-enabled income streams:**
- Client examinations booked through the marketplace.
- Paid peer consultations and mentorship (as available).
- Training, workshops, or educational products (future / subject to validation).
- Collaborations or referrals where permitted, transparently disclosed and compliant.

**User economics to evaluate:** gross revenue, transaction fees, acquisition costs, time spent participating, net incremental earnings, retained relationships, and client satisfaction. Never claim that participation automatically creates additional income. Subscription and commission pricing belong to the Product Brief; do not assume members will pay without demonstrated ROI.

## 14. Geographic and multilingual implications

- **Ukraine and Spain** are the first intentional market clusters; dense local supply and effective introductions matter more than immediate worldwide coverage.
- Discovery and profiles should accommodate local-language searches while allowing qualified cross-border connections where permitted.
- Translation may lower interaction friction but cannot substitute for professional accuracy, privacy and appropriate moderation.
- A globally available website is **not** equivalent to a liquid global marketplace.
- Validate the usefulness of international community participation separately from consumer demand for in-person examinations.

## 15. Trust, safety and ethics of professional content

Detelgy should avoid presenting unverified assertions as proof of credentials or competence. Profile verification, certifications, membership recognition, fulfilled marketplace orders, and reviews are **different trust signals** with separate meanings.

- Encourage anonymization and moderation of case discussions; do not expose client-identifying or sensitive case details.
- Do not promise perfect truth detection or universally accepted scientific validity. Polygraph use, accuracy claims, and legal contexts vary and require careful, sourced treatment.
- Expert-derived learning content should not be presented as universally certified guidance absent review.
- Reviews, consulting transactions and level achievements should use transparently defined criteria and anti-abuse controls.

## 16. Expert Discovery — research questions

Interview **growth-oriented practitioners** first to validate primary ICP, while interviewing established experts in parallel as potential community anchors. Prioritize behavior and recent specific experiences over abstract feature wishlists.

### Current reality
1. Walk me through where your last three new client inquiries came from.
2. Tell me about the most recent time you needed help from another examiner. What happened?
3. How do people currently verify your credentials or decide to work with you?
4. Which professional groups, associations, or events do you actually use, and how often?
5. What is the hardest part of growing your practice today?

### Alternatives and costs
6. Which tools or relationships already solve these problems adequately?
7. What do you currently pay for marketing, memberships, referrals, training, or professional advice?
8. Which activity takes more time than it should?

### Potential value
9. What would make a professional network worth returning to each month?
10. Have you ever paid for a peer consultation, mentor, or specialist training? What triggered that?
11. Would you publish an anonymized case or professional article? What would stop you?
12. What is the most valuable international connection you have made, if any?

### Adoption and economics
13. What would have to happen before you recommended such a platform to a colleague?
14. What would have to happen before you paid for a membership? What would make it not worth paying for?
15. What information about you should never be public?
16. Which of these outcomes matters most in the next 12 months: better skills, stronger reputation, more peers, more clients, or added income? Why?

**Research protocol:** capture verbatim evidence where participants consent; distinguish observations from interpretations; record participant role, country, practices, current alternatives, frequency, severity and willingness to pay. Avoid inventing quotes or using real names without permission.

## 17. Hypothesis validation backlog

| Hypothesis | Current confidence | Best initial test | Evidence that challenges it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent examiners want an additional trusted source of qualified demand | Unvalidated | Recent-inquiry interviews; first qualified leads | Existing channels fully satisfy needs; no attribution |
| Experts need more cross-country peer interaction | Unvalidated | Curated introductions and roundtables | Low repeat attendance; geography rarely matters |
| Contributing knowledge increases perceived reputation | Unvalidated | Track contribution-to-profile interest and qualitative feedback | No meaningful reputation effect |
| Industry Leaders will contribute without high short-term payments | Unvalidated | Invite pilot leaders with clear benefits | Low commitment / insufficient exchange value |
| Professionals pay for peer consultation and mentorship | Unvalidated | Concierge pilots with real prices | Interest but no actual purchases |
| Multilingual participation improves access and value | Unvalidated | Cross-language engagement experiments | Users remain entirely in local-language clusters |
| A free community can activate the client marketplace | Unvalidated | Test referral/inquiry conversion and buyer interviews | Engagement without buyer demand |

The market slide should use independently sourced, dated market evidence. Do not transform qualitative hypotheses into statistically representative claims.

## 18. Presentation handoff rules for Codex

**Core narrative:** *Skilled professionals may be invisible, isolated or constrained by disconnected tools. Detelgy aims to connect expertise, reputation, colleagues and commercial opportunities in one professional ecosystem.*

**Single-slide concepts worth visualizing:**
- Three complementary professional archetypes, with the Growth-Oriented Examiner as primary ICP.
- “Life today” as scattered tools/networks and a fragmented professional identity.
- Four jobs: Become Better / Become Known / Find Opportunities / Monetize Expertise.
- Transformation: **Connect → Learn → Build Reputation → Earn → Lead** (illustrative paths, not mandatory stages).
- Before/After comparison focused on professional outcomes, avoiding guaranteed revenue claims.
- Journey that branches at meaningful first-value/Aha Moments per persona.
- Value loop: useful participation → trust and relationships → opportunities → authentic outcomes → more useful participation (hypothesis to validate).

**Do not:**
- Fabricate demographic research, interviews, market size, user numbers, testimonials, revenue or measurable uplift.
- Use a stock-photo archetype as if it were an actual Detelgy member.
- Present future Academy, paid mentorship or marketplace demand as already proven at scale.
- Equate platform verification, visibility or badges with scientific proof of polygraph validity.
- Treat government examiners as automatically eligible to sell services.

**Suggested slide headlines (English):**
1. “Great expertise deserves greater opportunity.”
2. “A global profession. A fragmented journey.”
3. “One primary ICP. Three complementary success paths.”
4. “Expertise alone does not guarantee visibility.”
5. “The job is progress, not another platform.”
6. “From isolated specialist to connected professional.”
7. “The first value is different for every expert.”
8. “When experts succeed, the ecosystem grows.”
